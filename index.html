How the Bucket Strategy Works

The biggest risk to your retirement isn't a market crash — it's a crash right before you retire. If the market drops 37% at age 64 and you need the money at 65, you're forced to sell at the worst possible time. The bucket strategy prevents this.

Starting 5-7 years before retirement, you gradually shift your money into three buckets:
BUCKET 1 — CASH (1-2 years of expenses)
High-yield savings or money market. You live on this during a crash. Never touch your investments when they're down.
BUCKET 2 — BONDS (3-5 years of expenses)
Bond index funds. Stable during stock crashes. Refills Bucket 1 as you spend it. Gives your stocks time to recover.
BUCKET 3 — STOCKS (everything else)
Stock index funds for long-term growth. Even at 65, you could live 25+ more years — you still need growth. If a crash hits, Buckets 1 and 2 buy you 5-7 years for stocks to recover.

In this model: When enabled, the simulator gradually shifts your allocation from 100% stocks to ~40% stocks / 60% bonds between your start age and retirement. This reduces crash impact by absorbing losses in the bond portion while stocks recover.

The tradeoff: Less growth in the final years (bonds return ~4% vs stocks ~8%). But protection from a 2008-style crash right before retirement can save you $100,000+ compared to being 100% in stocks.

Social Security Quarters

Who is this for? International workers, immigrants, and green card holders tracking their path to Social Security eligibility.

Minimum to qualify: 40 credits (roughly 10 years of US work). This applies to all workers, including US citizens.

35-year calculation: SS uses your highest 35 earning years to calculate benefits. Fewer than 35 years means $0 is used for the missing years, reducing your monthly benefit.

US citizens abroad: Eligible to collect benefits in most countries regardless of years worked, as long as the 40-credit minimum is met.

International workers abroad: Benefits may stop after 6 months depending on citizenship and whether your country has a totalization agreement with the US. See the Leave US tab for details.

Known Limitations

This tool uses mathematical models and historical patterns to project possible outcomes. All projections are estimates. Specific limitations include:

Moderate impact: No behavioral risk modeling (assumes you hold through crashes). SS taxation simplified (uses 85% always). Healthcare costs are flat by age. No estate tax for non-citizens ($60k vs $13.6M exemption).

Low impact: Volatility scaling is approximate. Single inflation rate (no category-specific). Dual-state tax is simplified. Total employer+employee cap ($72k) not enforced.

Out of scope: Divorce, disability, annuities, cryptocurrency, estate planning, state-specific Medicaid rules. These require professional advice specific to your situation.
